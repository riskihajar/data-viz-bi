Kanformer: an attention‑enhanced deep learning model for predicting
student performance in virtual learning environments
Bayan Alnasyan1,2 · Mohammed Basheri1 · Madini Alassafi1 · Khozama Alnasyan2

Abstract
With the rapid growth of digital education, massive open online courses (MOOCs) have gained widespread popularity. How-
ever, high failure rates and low completion rates remain challenges, highlighting the need for accurate student performance
prediction to identify at-risk students and implement timely interventions. Many existing models assume simplistic or linear
dependencies, failing to capture the complex relationships and cross-dimensional interactions between different student
features. Therefore, KANFormer model is developed, a novel deep learning (DL) architecture that integrates multi-head self
attention (MHSA) mechanisms with Kolmogorov–Arnold networks (KAN) to effectively captures complex patterns within
large-scale student datasets, enabling more accurate predictions of student performance. KANFormer processes student demo-
graphic, academic, and engagement features from the open university learning analytics dataset (OULAD) while applying a
feature elimination process to enhance dataset quality, reduce redundancy, and mitigate multicollinearity, ensuring optimal
predictive performance. The model is evaluated on both binary and multi-class classification tasks, distinguishing successful
students (“Distinction” & “Pass”) from unsuccessful ones (“Fail” & “Withdrawn”), while also predicting individual per-
formance categories. Ablation experiments analyze the impact of MHSA and KAN on model performance. Experimental
results on the OULA dataset, comprising over 29,000 samples, demonstrate that KANFormer outperforms traditional and
state-of-the-art models, achieving superior accuracy, precision, and F1-score in student performance prediction. To enhance
interpretability, SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values are utilized to quantify each feature’s contribution to predic-
tions. SHAP analysis shows that academic assessments (exam scores, TMAs, CMAs) are the most influential predictors of
student success, while engagement indicators play a secondary role. Furthermore, the model’s generalization ability is vali-
dated on additional datasets, including ASSISTments2012, xAPI, and the Mathematics and Portuguese Language Courses
dataset, proving its scalability and robustness across different educational contexts. By accurately identifying at-risk students,
KANFormer offers a scalable, interpretable, and highly effective solution for improving student retention and success in
online education, providing actionable insights for personalized learning interventions.
